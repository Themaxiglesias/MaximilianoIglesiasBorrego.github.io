04 / MECHANICAL DESIGN

Cargo Tricycle Rear Axle

Design and analysis of a 900 mm rear axle for a cargo tricycle, including static strength, fatigue, deflection, bearing selection, and a torque-limiting key.

2,500 N Design load200 N·m Operating torque240 N·m Key failure target

From loading requirements to validation

Our team selected cold-rolled AISI 1018 steel, built a free-body diagram, calculated bending and torsional stresses, and checked fatigue and static safety factors. We modeled the shaft in Siemens NX and compared analytical deflection to ANSYS results.

The report documents a fatigue factor of safety of approximately 2.5 and static safety factor just under 2. It also outlines SKF bearing selection and a 9 × 4 × 16 mm key intended to fail at the specified overload torque.

Collaborative academic project. Report describes a proposed design and machining plan; it does not establish that the axle was physically manufactured.

05 / FINITE ELEMENT ANALYSIS

Stress Concentration Around a Circular Hole

ANSYS structural simulation of a tension-loaded steel plate, measuring how hole geometry affects stress concentration and comparing the results with theoretical values.

1,000 N Tensile load3 ratios d/w studied2.75 / 2.70 FEA / theory at d/w = 0.10

Checking simulation against theory

I created plate geometries in ANSYS, applied a 1,000 N tensile load, refined the mesh around the hole, and used peak stress divided by nominal stress to calculate stress concentration factors.

Across hole-to-width ratios of 0.01, 0.10, and 0.50, the study reported FEA factors of 2.99, 2.75, and 2.18, versus theoretical values of 2.90, 2.70, and 2.15, respectively.

Academic structural simulation, not physical testing. The uploaded Crowdmark PDF shows a 100/100 report grade.

06 / EXPERIMENTAL FLUID MECHANICS

Air Diffuser Performance

Experimental evaluation of diffuser angle, static pressure recovery, efficiency, and separated flow within a converging-diverging duct.

0°–30° Angles tested5° Test increments≈ 10° Peak pressure recovery

Finding the balance between expansion and separation

Our ME 333L team used wall pressure taps, Validyne transducers, and Pitot measurements to examine diffuser flow behavior. We studied how increasing the expansion angle affects pressure recovery and the onset of flow separation.

The report identifies maximum static pressure recovery near 10°, with separation and losses increasing at larger angles. It also notes that the peak-efficiency angle likely occurs slightly below 10° and recommends finer angle increments for follow-up tests.

Collaborative laboratory project; outcomes reflect the experimental report rather than a production diffuser design.
